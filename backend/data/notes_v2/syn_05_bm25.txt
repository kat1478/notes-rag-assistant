BM25 (Best Matching 25) is the ranking function used by most classic search engines, including Elasticsearch and Lucene by default. It can be seen as an improved TF-IDF. Two ideas make it better. First, term frequency saturation: in plain TF-IDF a word that appears 20 times counts twice as much as one that appears 10 times, but in BM25 the contribution of term frequency grows quickly at first and then flattens out. The parameter k1 controls how fast it saturates; typical values are between 1.2 and 2.0. Second, document length normalization: a long document naturally contains more words, so BM25 divides by the document length relative to the average document length. The parameter b controls how strong this normalization is; b = 0.75 is the usual default, b = 0 turns length normalization off and b = 1 applies it fully. The IDF part is similar to TF-IDF: rare terms get a higher weight than common ones. BM25 is a lexical method, so it only matches exact words; it does not know that "car" and "automobile" mean the same thing. That is why modern retrieval systems often combine BM25 with dense embeddings in a hybrid search.
