Regularization is a set of techniques that reduce overfitting by limiting how closely a model can fit the training data. L2 regularization (ridge, or weight decay in neural networks) adds lambda times the sum of squared weights to the loss. It pushes all weights towards small values but rarely makes them exactly zero. L1 regularization (lasso) adds lambda times the sum of absolute weights instead; because of the shape of the absolute value it drives many weights to exactly zero, so it also works as automatic feature selection. The strength lambda is a hyperparameter: too small and nothing changes, too large and the model underfits. Dropout is a regularization method specific to neural networks: during training each neuron is switched off with probability p (0.5 is a classic value for hidden layers), so the network cannot rely on any single neuron and effectively trains an ensemble of thinner networks. At inference time dropout is turned off. Early stopping is the simplest technique: you monitor the validation loss and stop training when it stops improving for a few epochs (the patience), keeping the best weights. More training data is also a form of regularization, which is why data augmentation, such as flipping or cropping images, helps.
