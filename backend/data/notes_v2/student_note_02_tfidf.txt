TF-IDF stands for Term Frequency-Inverse Document Frequency and it is a classic technique to turn text into numbers that a computer can understand. The whole point is to figure out which words actually matter in a document. TF (Term Frequency) is simple – it’s just how many times a word appears in a specific text. But that's not enough because common words like 'the' or 'and' appear everywhere. That is why we use IDF (Inverse Document Frequency). We calculate IDF as the logarithm of the total number of documents divided by the number of documents containing that specific term. This log function is crucial because it dampens the effect of high-frequency words. When you multiply TF by IDF, you get a score where high values mean the word is both frequent in your document and rare in the rest of the collection. This is way better than a simple Bag of Words count, which doesn't care about word importance. For example, if you have 100 documents and a word appears in 10 of them, your IDF is log(10). If that word shows up 5 times in your target doc, you just multiply that 5 by the log value to get the final relevance score. It has nothing to do with football world cups, just pure statistics.
